01 — THE PRODUCT

A REPORTING TOOL, NOT A CAMERA FEED

P0-Safety takes any construction-site image and reports whether the people in it are PPE-compliant. It does not need to run in real time and does not try to. You point it at a batch of site photographs and it returns a compliance assessment for each — usable as a dated safety record, not a notebook demo.

THE PRODUCT DECISION THAT MATTERS

The model does not find PPE and infer absence from silence. It is trained to detect no-helmet, no-gloves, no-boots and no-vest as things in their own right. A compliance report needs a positive assertion of non-compliance, not the absence of an assertion.

LEVER 1+0.204
DON'T TRAIN FROM SCRATCH

Random weights: 14.4 hours for 0.606. The same architecture from COCO-pretrained weights: 0.810. Unglamorous, and the largest single gain in the project.

LEVER 2+0.055
USE A CURRENT ARCHITECTURE

YOLOv8-n → YOLO26-s on identical data: 0.810 → 0.865, and +0.091 at strict IoU. The newer architecture localises better, not just classifies better — it draws tighter boxes. Training fell to 1.3 hours.

LEVER 3THE DATA
THE DATA IS THE BOTTLENECK

Merging three sources beat every single source: 0.883, then 0.898 at medium scale. Then we audited the merged dataset properly — and found the data was still the bottleneck in ways no metric had shown us.

03 — THE AUDIT · THE MOST VALUABLE THING WE FOUND

WE SPENT FOUR DAYS IMPROVING THE MODEL AND ONE AFTERNOON AUDITING THE DATA. THE AFTERNOON TAUGHT US MORE.

One pass over every label file, a perceptual-hash sweep of all 13,284 images and per-person coverage checks. It took seconds to run and invalidated most of what we thought we knew. Five findings, in order of damage.

FINDING 1 — CRITICAL
THE VALIDATION SPLIT IS CONTAMINATED

A 144-bit dhash sweep found 98,687 cross-split near-duplicate pairs, 3,242 of them hash-identical. The CCTV source was split by frame, not by camera or session — adjacent frames are near-identical. The model was being tested largely on pictures it had already seen.

FINDING 2 — CRITICAL
THE NEGATIVE CLASSES WERE NEVER ANNOTATED

Each source annotated only its own subset of the nine classes. Merging under a union label space turned every unannotated class into a silent false negative. On four images in five, a correct no-vest prediction is punished — the loss function was teaching the model to suppress the class we most wanted. Structural, not annotator error: a direct consequence of the merge that produced our best model.

FINDING 3 — HIGH
THE ONLY SOURCE OF NO-VEST IS 42.6% MOSAIC COMPOSITES

1,140 of that source's 2,679 images are tiled composites of unrelated photographs — boxes span tile seams and land on a bridge deck, a truck wheel, a phone screen. The entire supply of no-vest, all 4,158 instances, is drawn substantially on images where the geometry no longer matches the content.

FINDING 5 — MEDIUM
OFF-DOMAIN IMAGERY

An office worker at a CRT monitor, a drummer, a singer — each labelled no-helmet. The model was being taught that any bare head in any setting is a PPE breach: a direct attack on precision in the field.

FINDING 4 — HIGH
ONLY 1.9% OF PEOPLE HAVE A COMPLETE PPE DECISION

Class counts looked healthy — boots alone has 10,934 instances. Yet 80.9% of people have no boots decision at all. Only a per-person measure exposes this, and per-person is exactly what the product has to do at inference.

THE METHOD, WHICH GENERALISES
Reusable on any merged detection dataset. The order matters. Runs in seconds, so it doubles as a regression test after every fix.
  1. 1Build the source × class matrix first. One pass over the label files; every zero is a structural blind spot.
  2. 2Measure pair-decision rate, not class counts.
  3. 3Anchor every check on the person box. One labelled person in a crowd makes an image look finished.
  4. 4Validate the split before trusting any metric. On video-derived data this is not optional.
  5. 5Only now open images — filtered ones. Twelve targeted images beat two hundred random ones.
  6. 6Keep a random baseline sheet too. The mosaics and off-domain images surfaced from it.
  7. 7Re-run after every fix and diff the report.

WE SHIPPED THE MODEL WITH THE LOWER SCORE

mAP@500.900 ▲ mAP@50-950.606 ▲ person AP@500.919 ▲ person predicted on background0.31 — doubled

Adding 16,308 person annotations won on the leaderboard and lost in the confusion matrix: the false-positive person rate almost doubled. For a compliance report that is the wrong trade — a missed detection is a gap in coverage, but a false detection is a compliance flag raised against nobody, and a report full of phantom workers gets ignored.

THE HONEST FOOTNOTE
The person pass behind the rejected model is itself incomplete — 1,225 images still carry PPE boxes with no person box, so some of that 0.31 may be the dataset being wrong rather than the model. Both readings support the same decision; only a clean re-split settles which is true.

05 — WHAT WE'D DO NEXT

THEN RE-MEASURE, AND FIND OUT WHAT WE ACTUALLY BUILT

CRITICAL
RE-SPLIT BY CAMERA SESSION

Group on clip prefix so no camera contributes to both train and validation. This is the fix that makes every other number meaningful.

CRITICAL
MASK THE LOSS PER SOURCE

Stop punishing correct predictions of classes a source never annotated — score each class only where a label could exist.

HIGH
REBUILD THE MOSAIC SOURCE

Regenerate from the 2,801 base images and discard the baked-in composite augmentations no-vest currently trains on.

HIGH
MEASURE COMPLETENESS PER PERSON

Adopt items-decided-per-person as the dataset metric, annotate the gaps, and cull the off-domain imagery.
